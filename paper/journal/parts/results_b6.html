6.6 The output guard and the rate limiter, demonstrated

Both layers were exercised on the live gateway with the real filter and embedder. For the output guard, a cached answer carrying a browser script that sends the user's cookies to a third party was planted, and a reworded question was asked in each of the guard's three modes. With the guard off the script was served from the cache (status {{mit_guard_off}}); in flag mode it was served with a response header naming what was found ({{mit_guard_flag}}); in block mode the answer was withheld ({{mit_guard_block}}). The guard inspects the answer rather than the question, so it acts on a cache hit the input filter never saw, which makes it an independent second line behind the cache.

For the rate limiter, with a limit of {{mit_limit}} requests a minute, one caller sent every rewording of one attack three times in a single burst ({{mit_sent}} requests) at a planted poisoned answer. {{mit_answered}} were answered and {{mit_blocked}} blocked by the filter within the limit, and the remaining {{mit_refused}} were refused with status 429, while a second caller at the same moment was served normally (status {{mit_other}}). The limiter does not stop the attack; the attacker still received {{mit_poison}} poisoned answers inside the first minute. What it does is slow the probing of the cache to a fixed number of attempts per key per minute, which turns the cheap, rapid search for a matching rewording into a slow one.
