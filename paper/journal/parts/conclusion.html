8. Conclusion and Future Work

A semantic cache placed in front of a prompt-injection filter, in the order that performance demands, is a path around the filter. On a running gateway built from common open components, {{w4_lazy_85}}% of trivially reworded attacks retrieved a poisoned answer at a typical threshold, the gateway's own log recorded that the filter never ran, and no threshold value was both safe and useful. Storing the filter's verdict with each cached answer and filtering the search on it stops the easy case at no measurable cost but not the realistic one, in which the poison was stored as clean; re-checking only the hits that look risky does not close the gap either, because a cheap label cannot recognise most real attacks. Re-checking every hit removed every cache-caused leak, kept {{p3_all_hits}}% of honest cache hits, and cost tens of milliseconds against the seconds and money of the model call a hit avoids. What remains is the filter's own error, which we measured separately: almost no false alarms on ordinary text, but many on adversarial look-alikes and weak detection of conversational attacks. A cache defence can remove the risk the cache adds; it cannot repair the filter.

Several directions follow from these results:

  • Other filters and embedders. Repeating the measurements with other injection classifiers, LLM-based guards and embedding models would show how the absolute rates move while, we expect, the structural result holds.
  • The other injection routes. Pre-filled and cross-tenant caches, discussed but not measured here, would let a poisoned entry carry any verdict an attacker chooses, which makes re-checking hits even more important.
  • Adaptive attackers. An attacker who knows that every hit is re-checked will look for rewordings that match the poisoned entry and also evade the filter; combining the verdict re-check with an answer-validity check (Zhang, Yao, et al., 2026; Liang et al., 2026) is a natural next layer.
  • A learned model picker, evaluated on questions it was not tuned on, in place of the rule-based baseline.

Because every number in this paper is regenerated from committed logs by one command, each of these directions can be pursued as an extension of the same harness rather than a re-implementation.
