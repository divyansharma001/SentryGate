LLM gateways combine caching with other services. Cost-aware routing sends easy prompts to cheaper models, from cascades (Chen, Zaharia, & Zou, 2023) to routers learned from preference data (Ong et al., 2025); our model picker is a rule-based baseline in the same space. Industry guidance treats the components as separate risks: the OWASP Top 10 for LLM applications lists prompt injection first and vector and embedding weaknesses separately (OWASP Foundation, 2024).

Taken together, prior work establishes that semantic caches can be poisoned and that cache reuse can carry a stale decision forward. What we have not found is a measurement of the specific interaction that arises when a semantic cache is placed in front of a separate input safety classifier in a gateway, so that every hit skips the classifier; a measurement that reads the skip from the system's own logs and separates the leaks the cache causes from the classifier's own misses; or an evaluation of storing the classifier's verdict with each entry and re-checking hits as the fix, including its ablation and its cost under load. These are the contributions of this paper. The answer-validity checks of Zhang, Yao, et al. (2026) and Liang et al. (2026) re-check hits for a different property, whether the answer fits the query; the two kinds of check are complementary.
