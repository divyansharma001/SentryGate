A semantic cache stores past prompts as embeddings and returns a stored answer when a new prompt is close enough in meaning. GPTCache is the open-source reference design that most deployments follow (Bang, 2023); GPT Semantic Cache (Regmi & Pun, 2024), SCALM (Li et al., 2024) and MeanCache (Gill et al., 2025) refine it for hit rate, cost, latency and privacy. These systems are evaluated as performance components, and all decide a hit from embedding similarity alone. Schroeder et al. (2026) show that a single global threshold cannot bound the rate of wrong hits and replace it with learned per-entry thresholds. Their observation, made about answer correctness, is the performance counterpart of our result in Section 6.1 that no threshold is safe: a threshold controls how close two prompts are, not whether the stored answer is appropriate for the new one.
