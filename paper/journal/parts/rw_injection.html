Prompt injection was named and demonstrated by Perez and Ribeiro (2022), whose handcrafted "ignore previous instructions" prompts hijacked GPT-3's goal or leaked its prompt; the seed attack of our demonstration follows their phrasing. Greshake et al. (2023) extended the threat to indirect injection through content the model later reads, and showed real applications compromised. Liu et al. (2024) formalised attacks and defences and benchmarked them across models and tasks, and Yi et al. (2025) built a benchmark for the indirect case. Defences fall into two broad groups. Model-side defences change how the model treats untrusted text, by separating instructions from data (Chen, Piet, et al., 2025), preference optimisation (Chen, Zharmagambetov, et al., 2025) or marking untrusted input (Hines et al., 2024). System-side defences place a separate guard around the model: input and output classifiers such as Llama Guard (Inan et al., 2023), programmable rails (Rebedea et al., 2023), and dedicated injection detectors such as the DeBERTa-v3 classifier we deploy (He et al., 2023; ProtectAI.com, 2024), alongside simpler baselines such as perplexity filtering and paraphrasing (Jain et al., 2023). Detectors are known to be evadable: Hackett et al. (2025) bypass six commercial and open detectors with character-level and adversarial perturbations. Our result is complementary. We do not attack the detector; we show that a cache in front of it can remove it from the request path altogether, so even a perfect detector would not be consulted.
